How can Regenerating Planaria be Affected by Fluctuating Intensities of Underwater Currents?

Tudor Barcan, Reagan Lafnitzegger

Background

Planaria are flatworms with the capabilities of fully regenerating themselves.
Previous studies show:

  • Stem cell responses
  • Tissue reconstruction and programmed cell death
  • Moving stem cells toward the injury location while healing DNA
  • Brain Regeneration

Rational

Studying water current explores a real environmental aspect.
Regeneration requires the movement of cells:

  • Could be affected by an underwater current
  • Currents could cause mechanical stress on healing tissues

Goal: Test how varying intensities of underwater currents can influence planarian regeneration (growth in length) from the tail to the head

Hypothesis

Hypothesis: When subjected to increased water flow speeds, planarian tail ends will regenerate at different rates compared to fragments from planarians placed in still water over four days.

Prediction: Planarian tail ends subjected to the highest tested underwater current (13 cm/s) would have the highest detectable difference in tail growth compared to those in still water.
